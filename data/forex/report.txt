The global monetary situation in early April 2026 shows a complex interplay between inflationary pressures, geopolitical tensions and shifting risk sentiment. The ongoing Iran war has introduced substantial volatility across financial markets, with energy prices climbing sharply and global supply chains under strain. Central banks, particularly the U.S. Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank, have responded cautiously, balancing the need to maintain price stability against the risks posed by prolonged conflict and sanctions. As a result, global monetary conditions remain tight, with liquidity constrained in financial markets and investors increasingly seeking diversification and safety amid rising uncertainty.

G10 major currencies represent the most liquid and influential group in the forex market. The U.S. Dollar (USD), Euro (EUR), and Japanese Yen (JPY) are the most prominent, driven by the Federal Reserve’s cautious stance on interest rates, the European Central Bank’s efforts to stabilize inflation, and the Bank of Japan’s continued monetary easing. The USD remains under pressure due to the impact of the Iran war on global oil prices and the potential for increased U.S. military spending. The EUR is supported by the ECB's focus on maintaining price stability, despite the ongoing energy crisis and its influence on European inflation. The JPY has been a safe-haven asset, with yen carry trades and a dovish stance from the BOJ contributing to its relative strength.

Commodity-linked currencies, dominated by the Australian Dollar (AUD), Canadian Dollar (CAD), and New Zealand Dollar (NZD), reflect the broader economic outlook on global commodity markets. The AUD and CAD have seen considerable appreciation due to heightened oil prices and strong demand for energy resources, while the NZD, linked to agricultural exports, has remained mixed with concerns over global food supply chain disruptions and falling commodity prices in some sectors. These currencies are sensitive to swings in energy and natural resource demand, and their performance is closely tied to the geopolitical environment and the health of global trade.

Emerging market high-yield currencies, including the Mexican Peso (MXN), South African Rand (ZAR), and Brazilian Real (BRL), face a more precarious position. The MXN is under strain due to trade balance issues exacerbated by the U.S.-Iran conflict, with Mexico’s dependence on oil exports and its ties to U.S. markets creating additional exposure. The ZAR is volatile amid concerns over the broader African economic climate and the effects of sanctions on the Russian oil market. The BRL is influenced by Brazil’s strategic position in global commodity markets and the potential for Brazilian economic reforms to bolster investor confidence.

Safe-haven assets, primarily represented by the Swiss Franc (CHF), Gold (XAU/USD), and Japanese Yen (JPY), continue to see inflows as investors seek refuge from market turbulence. The CHF remains a strong contender due to its reputation as a stable currency and the Swiss National Bank’s policy of maintaining a strong franc to protect against inflation. The XAU/USD, while traditionally a safe-haven asset, has shown weakness amid an unexpected shift in market sentiment, as the Fed's rate hikes are seen as a stabilization tool rather than a risk-escalating factor. The JPY, alongside the USD, has attracted safe-haven demand, with the BOJ continuing to provide liquidity support through monetary easing and Japan’s generally stable economic conditions.

The interactions between G10 currencies, commodity-linked currencies, emerging markets, and safe-haven assets reflect a dynamic and often conflicting global market environment. Interest rate differentials play a key role in capital flows, with the Fed’s potential for further rate hikes drawing from U.S. economic strength and the GDP growth trajectory, while the ECB and BOJ maintain accommodative policies to support their economies. As the Iran war escalates, risk sentiment swings between fear and optimism, influencing the flow of capital between emerging market currencies and safe-haven assets. Market participants increasingly look to trade balance and monetary policy trajectories to assess the relative value of these currencies for investment and hedging purposes. The structural weaknesses in emerging market economies, compounded by capital flight fears, make them more susceptible to external shocks, whereas the G10 currencies benefit from their deep liquidity and more resilient economic structures. The commodity-linked currencies, meanwhile, are caught between rising prices and the risk of supply chain disruptions, leading to increased volatility. The current state of the market seems to reflect a mix of historical patterns and current developments, with the outcomes dependent on the trajectory of the Iran war and the effectiveness of monetary policy responses.
